# Monster: hints (rules from the references)

## Take
1. **Show less than you have.** Kohada Koheiji is a hand and a head over a net. Our reveal ladder starts with sound and ends with the full body only once (the director: "people's imagination is scarier... hinted at but appear later").
2. **A human body, held wrong.** (Amnesia) Not a new animal: two elbows per arm, the second bending backwards, head below the shoulder line, head rolling past 80 degrees.
3. **Motion is the design.** (butoh, the uncanny article) Long stillness, then a 2–3 frame snap. Never a smooth walk cycle at full view. Stepped head keys in StalkWalk.
4. **Half a face.** (Oiwa) One white eye-side, one burnt side. One red pupil is the only colour in the face.
5. **The space changes before she arrives.** (Shibata's "subtle change in the background") Lights, rain, wall shadows and sound shift first (H8).
6. **Silhouette must not be a tall thin man.** Crouched height about 1.2 m next to a 1.8 m human. Wide flat torso, very long arms, dragging hair.

## Don't copy
- No specific creature: not Sadako/Kayako (hair-crawl woman), not Pyramid Head, Lying Figure or Mannequin, not Amnesia's Grunt/Brute, not Fatal Frame's named ghosts, not Slender Man.
- Do not trace the Hokusai or Ōkyo compositions. They are grammar, not layouts.
- **Real atomic-bomb victims are off limits.** No Hiroshima or Nagasaki names, no recreating the Sumitomo Bank steps, no real victim's likeness. Our disaster and town are fictional; the flash-shadow is an abstract motif. Director to confirm the tone (see monster-design.md, decisions).
- Butoh is studied for timing and posture only. Never copy a performer's choreography beat for beat or put white body-paint on her.
