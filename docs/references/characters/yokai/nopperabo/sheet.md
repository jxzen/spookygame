## Folklore
- A yokai that looks human but has no face. It appears as an ordinary person, sometimes someone the victim knows, then its features vanish to a smooth blank. It frightens and is otherwise harmless. Often said to be a fox, tanuki or mujina in disguise. [fetched: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noppera-bō]
- The *recurrent spooking* motif (*saido no kai*): the victim flees the first faceless figure, finds help, and the helper turns out to be faceless too. [fetched: same]
- Lafcadio Hearn's "Mujina" (*Kwaidan*, 1904) is set on the Kii-no-kuni-zaka slope in Akasaka, Tokyo, by an old moat. A weeping woman "turned around, and dropped her sleeve, and stroked her face with her hand", and it was blank. The merchant runs to a soba seller's lantern; the seller strokes his own face, "Was it anything like THIS that she showed you?", and "simultaneously, the light went out." [fetched: gutenberg.org/files/1210/1210-h/1210-h.htm]
- Edo cases include a 2.1 m noppera-bo at Oike-cho, Kyoto (*Sorori Monogatari*, 1663). [fetched: Wikipedia, same]

| Where in our level | Beside a moat or canal; at a dead food stall under one lantern |
| Trigger | The player approaches within ~3 m, or uses an "interact" prompt on what looks like the mission's first living person |
| Duration | The turn and reveal take ~1.0 s. Then the player's light goes out (Hearn's ending) |
| Exit | Darkness for ~1.5 s. When the light returns, no one is there |
| Trace left | A dropped sleeve or a cloth on the wet ground |
| Sound cue | Soft weeping that stops mid-breath when the player is ~3 m away. Then the click of the light dying, and rain only |

## Do not
- **Not the egg-head Edo comic prints** (source/masasumi-noppera-bo-1853.jpg is kept as a bad example: an oval blank head on a dancing body reads as a joke). The blank face is skin with a hint of brow and nose structure under it, as if smoothed over.
- Use the double-scare (*saido no kai*) only once in the mission.
- No jump-scare scream; the silence and the light going out are the scare.
- Not a monster that chases. The real antagonist stays hinted (director, probe 03: "the actual monster should be hinted at but appear later on").
