## Folklore
- Looks almost completely human. One type's neck stretches; another's head comes off and flies about freely (nukekubi). Found in classical kaidan and yokai works. [fetched: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokurokubi]
- The *Sorori Monogatari* (1663) reads the wandering head as a sleeping woman's soul. A man chased a flying head with his sword into a house, and a voice inside said, "I had a scary dream. I was chased by a man with a sword." [fetched: same]
- Sekien's plate (*Gazu Hyakki Yagyo*, 1776) shows a sleeping woman whose head rises on a thin neck-line **over the top of a folding screen** (byobu), looking down. Hokusai's pair shows necks as long loops, one smoking a pipe. [fetched: source/ images, viewed]

## Do not
- **The neck must never show in full.** The Hokusai loops read playful (he drew one smoking a pipe). Show the head and a few metres of neck at most, then occlusion.
- No grinning face. Sekien's face is calm and asleep: keep it.
- Avoid the floating-head reading next to the chochin-obake, since two "faces at head height" in one mission blur together. Pick one of the two.
