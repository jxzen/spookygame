## Folklore
- A bald child with one eye in the centre of its forehead. Generally harmless: it appears suddenly to surprise people, and is "often depicted cutely, or in a humorous design". [fetched: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitotsume-kozō]
- *Kaidan Oi no Tsue*: in a samurai estate in Yotsuya, Edo, a boy of about ten rolled a hanging scroll up and down. Told to stop, he said "shut up" and turned, showing one eye. The visitor fainted. *Aizu Kaidan Shu*: a child of eight or nine asked a girl if she wanted money, then glared at her with its one eye, and she fainted. [fetched: same]
- Said to appear more often outside than inside. [fetched: same]
- Kitao Masayoshi's print (c.1788) shows it in a kimono, carrying a lantern, with clawed feet. [fetched: source/, viewed]

## Do not
- The one eye must never be seen clearly. Up close, this yokai reads comic or cute (Wikipedia says so).
- No tofu tray (the karuta tradition) and no tongue.
- Keep it far. If it ever comes close, cut it.
