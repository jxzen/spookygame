## Folklore
- Often treated as a kind of noppera-bo. Seen from behind, a beautiful woman in a kimono, sometimes in bridal wear, near shrines and temples at dusk. When approached she turns: a white, featureless face with a huge mouth of blackened teeth, and she cackles. It can also appear disguised as a man's own wife. Often blamed on a fox, tanuki or mujina. [fetched: yokai.com/ohagurobettari, which cites no sources]
- Takehara Shunsen's *Ehon Hyaku Monogatari* (1841) shows her crouched by a shrine's wooden fence with her face hidden under a cloth; below it, a wide black-toothed mouth and no eyes. [fetched: source/shunsen-haguro-bettari-1841.jpg, viewed]
- There is no English Wikipedia article (an API lookup on 2026-11-12 returned no page). The sourcing is thin.

## Do not
- Same reveal structure as the noppera-bo. **Use one or the other**; the noppera-bo has the better source (Hearn) and is ranked higher.
- *Ohaguro* (tooth-blackening) was a real cosmetic practice of married women and others until the Meiji period [recall]. Don't frame historical Japanese beauty practice as monstrous in itself. Sensitivity flag.
