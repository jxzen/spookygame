# Zashiki-warashi (座敷童子, "parlour child") · sheet

## Folklore
- Spirit children, told about mostly in Iwate Prefecture, who live in parlours or storerooms and play pranks. A house where one lives prospers. Recorded in Kunio Yanagita's *Tono Monogatari* (chapters 17-18). [fetched: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zashiki-warashi]
- Kizen Sasaki suggested they may be the spirits of children killed and buried under the house. Such spirits were said to walk the outer edges of the house **on rainy days**. [fetched: same]
- Usually known only by traces: children's footprints through ash, the sound of tops spinning, rustling paper, children's voices. A house it leaves falls into ruin. It looks about five or six years old. Sightings are rare, often only "a vague, child-like shape". [fetched: yokai.com/zashikiwarashi, which cites no sources]

| Sound cue | One child's footfall before the glimpse. In the empty room the player reaches, a wooden top spins down on the boards (a rattle slowing to a stop) |

## Do not
- Never show the face. No red-cheeked cute child, and no bobbed-hair "ghost girl" from J-horror films.
- Never refer to infanticide on screen (see the roster's sensitivity notes). The tradition can colour the tone; it is not content.
- No laughing-child audio sting. It is overused and reads cheap.
- In the folklore the figure is lucky, not hostile. Our reading: **the luck has left this town**. The child is leaving, not hunting.
