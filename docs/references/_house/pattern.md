# House rules as patterns

Each entry: name, problem, rule, reason, good example, bad example. The director's words are quoted verbatim.

## H1. Tone before build
- Problem: tone drifts when the director first sees it in a finished piece.
- Rule: before any first-of-kind build, ask the director clarifying questions about tone and show real media. Keep asking until the reference is picked.
- Reason: director, Q7: "ask for more input or clarifying questions earlier during the design of the game to get the tone right."
- Good: three reference readings shown before a single asset exists.
- Bad: a finished level that is the director's first look at the mood.

## H2. Fifteen-minute batches
- Problem: review fatigue makes verdicts sloppy.
- Rule: a review batch takes the director at most 15 minutes. Split anything larger.
- Reason: director, Q7: "a batch can take 15 minutes."
- Good: five three-ups on one page, one keystroke per pick.
- Bad: forty items on one page.

## H3. Epic openings
- Problem: none yet; this is a target.
- Rule: openings (of the game, and of missions if the core loop has them) are judged two-up against the director's picked title-sequence anchor.
- Reason: director, Q9: "make the game feel epic like a james bond intro".
- Good: to be set by the title-sequence pick.
- Bad: to be set by the first rejection.

## H4. The hero is the subject of the title sequence
- Problem: title sequences can drift into abstract imagery that doesn't show who we are.
- Rule: imagery stays clean and centred on the main character; the sequence works as a warm-up into the mission.
- Reason: director, batch 1 pick (Skyfall): "clean imagery with focus on main character. nice warmup. another good reference is archer s1 intro scene"
- Good: the director's pick, Skyfall main titles (docs/references/title-sequence/skyfall-main-titles).
- Bad: none yet.

## H5. Painted, not rendered
- Problem: stylised 3D often reads as digital art, which is the wrong medium for this game.
- Rule: surfaces read as painted backgrounds; characters read as flat animated shapes; colour is taken from the Spirited Away palette study.
- Reason: director, batch 1 pick (Spirited Away): "more animated cartoony style preferred. spirited away color pallete, and the painted medium over digital art is preferred."
- Good: docs/references/environment/spirited-away.
- Bad (rejected readings): Dishonored 2, Ghostwire: Tokyo. Their painterly but digital look was passed over.

## H6. The score travels with the spy
- Problem: a generic epic score ignores where the player is.
- Rule: an ominous orchestral base (Paul's Dream reading) plus solo instruments from the region the mission is set in.
- Reason: director, batch 1 pick: "the orchestra + foreign instruments based on where the american spy is lost in a foreign country would be really cool"
- Good: docs/references/audio/zimmer-pauls-dream.
- Bad: none yet.

## H7. Make it extra epic
- Reason: director, tone t6: "the theme is director's cut, so make it extra epic"
- Rule: when two options are otherwise equal, take the more cinematic one. Judged two-up against the title-sequence anchor.

## H5 (REVISED 2026-11-12). Monochrome dread, red accents, real shadows
- Problem: probe 01's painted, cartoon look was "not spooky enough".
- Rule: black, white and grey world; red is the only colour and marks danger, blood or the way forward. A light source casts real shadows, and darkness is the default. No cartoon outlines on the world.
- Reason: director, probe 01: "the spirited away aesthetic isnt spooky enough. can we switch to silent hill or alanwake style aesthetic with black/white/grey/red color scheme and shadows"; chat: "please change to be more scary and have less spirited away/cartoon vibe"
- Good: to be fetched and picked: Silent Hill 2 (remake) screenshots, Alan Wake 2 trailer frames.
- Bad: docs/anchors/environment/rejected/probe01-street.png

## H6 (REVISED). Strings plus koto
- Rule: an orchestral string base, with a Japanese koto for highlights and solos.
- Reason: director, probe 01: "keep the orchestra string instruments. add a spooky japanese koto highlights/solo"

## H8. The antagonist changes the level
- Rule: the antagonist's presence must alter the space (lights, fog, surfaces, sound), not only chase the player.
- Reason: director, probe 01: "bad guy needs to be more scary and impact the level."

## H9. Every probe is playable
- Rule: a probe has objectives, a threat and a fail state; it is never only a walk-through.
- Reason: director, probe 01: "no gameplay yet. expand upon it please"

## H10. The world shift: impossible architecture
- Rule: the dimension shift draws on three readings: the Inception city fold (space bends over the player), the Infinity Castle (endless Japanese interiors that reconfigure: rooms rotate and stack, floors become walls), and Backrooms (liminal, endless, humming rooms that feel wrong). It stays rare (director, t3).
- Reason: director, mid-probe-02 message: "for the world-shifting mechanic. use references of the inception movie, demon slayer newest movie w/ castle, backrooms movie. use silent hill graphics to make more spooky. have setting be in japan."
- Evidence: Inception clips fetched (vfx/inception-city-fold, vfx/inception-final-kick); Infinity Castle and Backrooms clips fetching 2026-11-12.

## H11. Hurt, don't end
- Rule: the first grab damages the player, then the monster backs away; the threat costs health, it does not reset the run.
- Reason: director: "first grab should damage you and then the monster backs away."

## H12. Horror first, spy second; the escape is the spy's moment
- Rule: horror carries the mission; the spy identity surfaces in the title and the ending. The mission ends with an escape aboard a shinkansen, scored as a spy-thriller cue.
- Reason: director: "end with escape on a japanese shinkansen bullet train with good spy music." and "the spy theme may not be as emphasized as the horror theme."

## H13. Near-blinding static
- Rule: static at full fear stays near-blinding (accessibility toggles still reduce it).
- Reason: director: "keep it near-blinding."
