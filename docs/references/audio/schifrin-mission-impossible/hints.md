# Hints: Schifrin, "Mission: Impossible"

## Mechanism rules we take
1. **An odd bar makes the pulse restless.** Five-beat bars (measured) never let the ear settle on a square downbeat: urgency without more tempo. Our spy cue is in 5/4 at 144 BPM, grouped 2+3+2+3 eighths (two off-beat accents), not this theme's long-long-short-short 3+3+2+2 (recall), so it does not quote it. Measured on our render: the 5-beat lag is the strongest bar lag (0.44-0.61 against 0.23-0.40 at 4 beats, Metre.cs with Smooth = 2), weaker than this recording on the same setting (1.22-1.32 in the mid and high bands, 0-30 s).
2. **The engine never stops.** One bass ostinato plus percussion carries the whole piece; melody arrives in bursts above it. Our spy cue: bass, kit and muted koto run continuously; brass and the leitmotif come and go on top.
3. **A pedal with chromatic neighbours.** A fixed bass with half-step neighbours (F#, C#, Eb measured high) gives tension without the harmony moving. We already own one: D with Eb, the horror score's dread note, so the spy bass leans D-Eb in its Eb/D bar.
4. **Loud and narrow.** LRA 6.2 LU: action music sits in a tight loudness band. Our spy cue sits a few LU above the horror average and stays flat.

## What not to copy
- The theme melody, the bass riff and its rhythm, the section figures, the bongo sound. No James Bond material either (docs/homage.md). Never ship the audio.
